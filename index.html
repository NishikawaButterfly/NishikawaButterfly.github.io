Electrical Engineer & Engineering Software Developer

I build tested software for data-center electrical systems, critical facilities, energy storage and quantitative analysis.

Public work published under NishikawaButterfly.

Most of my day job is around data centers and energy projects, and I write software to take the tedious parts out of that work. Everything public here has a tagged release, and all of the sample data is made up. Seven projects live on GitHub; three of them run as live demos you can open right now. These are the ones I'd show first.

Three places to start

dc-twin

Released · v0.2.0

A deterministic, capacity-constrained simulator for synthetic data-center electrical architectures.

The interesting bit: the same inputs and model version always reproduce the same SHA-256 result hash, and the reference scenarios match hand calculations committed independently of the engine.

Live demo · Repository

Electrical Asset Validator

Released · v0.1.0

A self-hosted web app that validates CSV and XLSX electrical asset registers and diffs two revisions by stable asset tag.

The interesting bit: both file formats go through exactly the same nine-column rule set, and every finding names the row and field it refers to.

Live demo · Repository

Critical Facilities Manager

Released · v0.1.0

An operations and commissioning platform for critical facilities behind a token-authenticated, role-guarded REST API.

The interesting bit: mutual-exclusion constraints stop simultaneous maintenance on redundant equipment — the second order is rejected with a 409 naming the constraint and the conflicting order.
